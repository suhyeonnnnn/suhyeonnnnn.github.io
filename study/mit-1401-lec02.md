    <!-- 1. Assumptions on Preferences -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">1. Three Key Assumptions on Preferences</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Assumption</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Meaning</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Implication</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Completeness</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Given two choices, consumers either prefer one, prefer the other, or are indifferent</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">No "I don't know" — consumers always have opinions</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Transitivity</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">If A ≻ B and B ≻ C, then A ≻ C</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Standard mathematical assumption</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Non-satiation</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">More is always better</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">If offered something for free, you'll always take it</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Note on Non-satiation:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Does NOT mean the 10th unit makes you as happy as the 9th</li>
                    <li>Just means the 10th unit is better than not having it</li>
                    <li>Applies to "goods" — things you want (not "bads" like getting poked in the eye)</li>
                </ul>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 2. Indifference Curves -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">2. Indifference Curves</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <p><strong>Definition:</strong> A curve connecting all bundles of goods that give the consumer the same level of satisfaction (utility).</p>

            <h4>Example: Pizza and Cookies</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <ul>
                    <li><strong>Choice A:</strong> 2 slices of pizza, 1 cookie</li>
                    <li><strong>Choice B:</strong> 1 slice of pizza, 2 cookies</li>
                    <li><strong>Choice C:</strong> 2 slices of pizza, 2 cookies</li>
                </ul>
                <p>If indifferent between A and B, but prefer C to both → A and B are on the same indifference curve, C is on a higher one.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Four Properties of Indifference Curves</h4>
            
            <!-- Property 1 -->
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0; border-left: 4px solid #2563eb;">
                <p style="font-weight: 600; color: #1e40af; margin-bottom: 0.5rem;">Property 1: Higher ICs are preferred</p>
                <p><strong>Reason:</strong> Non-satiation (more is better)</p>
                <p>IC₂ is farther from the origin than IC₁ → more pizza AND more cookies → IC₂ is preferred.</p>
            </div>

            <!-- Property 2 -->
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0; border-left: 4px solid #2563eb;">
                <p style="font-weight: 600; color: #1e40af; margin-bottom: 0.5rem;">Property 2: ICs are downward sloping</p>
                <p><strong>Reason:</strong> Non-satiation (can't be indifferent between less and more)</p>
                <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 0.75rem; border-radius: 4px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                    <p><strong>Proof by contradiction — If IC were upward sloping:</strong></p>
                    <p>Point A (1 pizza, 1 cookie) and Point B (3 pizza, 3 cookies) would be on the same IC.</p>
                    <p>→ You'd be indifferent between A and B.</p>
                    <p>→ But B has MORE of everything! How can you be indifferent? <strong>Contradiction!</strong></p>
                </div>
                <p>Therefore, to stay indifferent when gaining cookies, you must give up pizza → IC slopes downward.</p>
            </div>

            <!-- Property 3 -->
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0; border-left: 4px solid #2563eb;">
                <p style="font-weight: 600; color: #1e40af; margin-bottom: 0.5rem;">Property 3: ICs never cross</p>
                <p><strong>Reason:</strong> Transitivity + Non-satiation</p>
                <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 0.75rem; border-radius: 4px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                    <p><strong>Proof by contradiction — If two ICs crossed at point A:</strong></p>
                    <ul>
                        <li>A and B are on IC₁ → A ~ B (indifferent)</li>
                        <li>A and C are on IC₂ → A ~ C (indifferent)</li>
                        <li>By transitivity: B ~ C</li>
                    </ul>
                    <p><strong>But!</strong> If B has more pizza and same cookies as C → B ≻ C by non-satiation.</p>
                    <p>B ~ C AND B ≻ C? <strong>Contradiction!</strong></p>
                </div>
                <p>Therefore, indifference curves can never cross.</p>
            </div>

            <!-- Property 4 -->
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0; border-left: 4px solid #2563eb;">
                <p style="font-weight: 600; color: #1e40af; margin-bottom: 0.5rem;">Property 4: Only one IC through any point</p>
                <p><strong>Reason:</strong> Completeness (must know how you feel)</p>
                <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 0.75rem; border-radius: 4px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                    <p><strong>If two ICs passed through point A:</strong></p>
                    <ul>
                        <li>On IC₁: bundle A gives utility level U₁</li>
                        <li>On IC₂: bundle A gives utility level U₂</li>
                    </ul>
                    <p>Same bundle, two different utility levels? → You don't know how you feel about A.</p>
                    <p>This violates <strong>completeness</strong> — you must always be able to rank bundles!</p>
                </div>
                <p>Therefore, exactly one IC passes through each point.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Summary Table</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">#</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Property</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Key Intuition</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Higher ICs preferred</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">More is better</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Downward sloping</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">To gain one good, must give up the other to stay indifferent</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">3</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Never cross</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Crossing creates "B~A~C but B≻C" contradiction</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">One IC per point</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Each bundle has exactly one satisfaction level</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>Real-World Example: Job Choice</h4>
            <div style="background: #f5f5f5; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p>A graduate student choosing between jobs based on two dimensions:</p>
                <ul>
                    <li><strong>X-axis:</strong> Weather quality</li>
                    <li><strong>Y-axis:</strong> School/job quality</li>
                </ul>
                <p><strong>Options:</strong> Princeton (great quality, mediocre weather) vs. Santa Cruz (good quality, great weather)</p>
                <p><strong>Decision:</strong> Chose IMF (DC) — better weather than Princeton, better job than Santa Cruz</p>
                <p><em>Note: There's no "right" answer — preferences are personal.</em></p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 3. Utility Functions -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">3. Utility Functions</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <p><strong>Definition:</strong> A mathematical representation of preferences that assigns a number to each bundle of goods.</p>
            <p>The key idea: <strong>expressing indifference curves as equations</strong>!</p>

            <h4>Example: Square Root Utility</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.1rem; text-align: center;">U = √(S × C)</p>
                <p style="text-align: center;">where S = slices of pizza, C = cookies</p>
            </div>

            <p><strong>Verification:</strong></p>
            <ul>
                <li>U(2, 1) = √2 ≈ 1.41</li>
                <li>U(1, 2) = √2 ≈ 1.41 → Indifferent between A and B ✓</li>
                <li>U(2, 2) = 2 → Prefer C ✓</li>
            </ul>

            <h4>Why the √ Form?</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0; border-left: 4px solid #2563eb;">
                <p><strong>Student question:</strong> "U = S × C also gives the same ranking. Why use √?"</p>
                <p><strong>Answer:</strong> Different forms have different properties:</p>
                <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-family: monospace;">U = S × C</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Marginal utility is constant</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-family: monospace;">U = √(S × C)</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Marginal utility is <strong>diminishing</strong> ✓</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-family: monospace;">U = (S × C)²</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Marginal utility is increasing</td>
                    </tr>
                </table>
                <p>All three give the same <strong>ranking</strong>, but √ reflects real-world behavior: the 10th slice isn't as satisfying as the 1st.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Key Insight: Ordinal, Not Cardinal</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Utility is meaningful only for ranking, not for measuring absolute happiness.</strong></p>
                
                <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                    <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <th style="padding: 0.5rem; text-align: left;"></th>
                        <th style="padding: 0.5rem; text-align: left;">Cardinal (Wrong)</th>
                        <th style="padding: 0.5rem; text-align: left;">Ordinal (Correct)</th>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">Meaning</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Numbers have absolute meaning</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Only order matters</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">Example</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Temperature (30°C = 2×15°C)</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Movie rankings (1st > 2nd)</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">Utility</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">"U=6 is 2× happier than U=3" ❌</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">"U=6 > U=3 so prefer" ✓</td>
                    </tr>
                </table>

                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Key point:</strong> U = S×C, U = √(S×C), U = ln(S×C) all give the same ranking → they're all valid utility functions for the same preferences.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Verifying Same Ranking</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p>Compare bundles (2,3) vs (3,2):</p>
                <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                    <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <th style="padding: 0.5rem;">Utility Function</th>
                        <th style="padding: 0.5rem;">U(2,3)</th>
                        <th style="padding: 0.5rem;">U(3,2)</th>
                        <th style="padding: 0.5rem;">Preference?</th>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-family: monospace;">S × C</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">6</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">6</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Indifferent</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-family: monospace;">√(S × C)</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">2.45</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">2.45</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Indifferent</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-family: monospace;">(S × C)²</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">36</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">36</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Indifferent</td>
                    </tr>
                </table>
                <p><strong>Numbers differ, but ranking is identical!</strong></p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 4. Marginal Utility -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">4. Marginal Utility and Diminishing MU</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>What is Marginal Utility?</h4>
            <p><strong>Definition:</strong> The additional utility from consuming <strong>one more unit</strong> of a good.</p>
            
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Total Utility vs Marginal Utility:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li><strong>U(3)</strong> = Total satisfaction from 3 units</li>
                    <li><strong>MU(3)</strong> = Additional satisfaction from the 3rd unit = U(3) - U(2)</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>Mathematical Derivation</h4>
            <p>For U = √(S × C), we use partial derivatives:</p>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center;">
                    MU<sub>C</sub> = ∂U/∂C = S / (2√(S×C))
                </p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center;">
                    MU<sub>S</sub> = ∂U/∂S = C / (2√(S×C))
                </p>
            </div>

            <h4>What Does ∂U/∂C Mean?</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0; border-left: 4px solid #10b981;">
                <p><strong>Partial derivative</strong> = "Hold other variables constant, how does U change with C?"</p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Regular derivative (d/dx): only 1 variable</li>
                    <li>Partial derivative (∂/∂x): multiple variables, hold others constant</li>
                </ul>
                <p>The symbol ∂ (instead of d) means: <strong>treat S as a constant</strong>, then differentiate with respect to C.</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>MU<sub>C</sub></strong> = "If I hold pizza constant and eat one more cookie, how much happier am I?"</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Step-by-Step Calculation</h4>
            <div style="background: #f5f5f5; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace;">
                    U = (S × C)<sup>1/2</sup><br><br>
                    Treat S as constant, differentiate with respect to C:<br><br>
                    ∂U/∂C = (1/2) × S<sup>1/2</sup> × C<sup>-1/2</sup><br>
                    &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;= (1/2) × √S / √C<br>
                    &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;= (1/2) × √(S/C)<br>
                    &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;= S / (2√(S×C))
                </p>
            </div>

            <h4>Diminishing Marginal Utility ⭐</h4>
            <blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid #2563eb; padding-left: 1rem; margin: 1rem 0; color: #374151;">
                "More is better, but the next unit is not quite as good as the one before."
            </blockquote>

            <div style="background: #f0fdf4; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Intuitive Example — Hunger:</strong></p>
<pre style="font-family: monospace; margin: 0.5rem 0; background: #fff; padding: 0.5rem; border-radius: 4px;">
You haven't eaten all day. Pizza arrives!

1st slice: "Amazing!!!"     → MU = 😍😍😍😍😍 (5)
2nd slice: "Still great"    → MU = 😍😍😍😍 (4)
3rd slice: "Pretty good"    → MU = 😍😍😍 (3)
4th slice: "Getting full"   → MU = 😍😍 (2)
5th slice: "Can't eat more" → MU = 😍 (1)
</pre>
                <p><strong>Key:</strong> Total utility keeps increasing (5 slices > 4 slices), but marginal utility decreases.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Numerical Example: Holding Pizza at 2 Slices</h4>
            <p>With MU<sub>C</sub> = S / (2√(S×C)) and S = 2:</p>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Cookies (C)</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">√(S×C)</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Utility</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">MU<sub>C</sub></th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">√2 ≈ 1.41</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">1.41</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">0.71</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">√4 = 2.00</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">2.00</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">0.50</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">3</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">√6 ≈ 2.45</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">2.45</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">0.41</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">√8 ≈ 2.83</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">2.83</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">0.35</td>
                </tr>
            </table>
            <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280;">Utility ↑ but MU ↓ as cookies increase → <strong>Diminishing Marginal Utility</strong></p>

            <h4>Important Distinction</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Concept</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">As Quantity ↑</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Why?</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Utility (U)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Increases ↑</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Non-satiation (more is better)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Marginal Utility (MU)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Decreases ↓</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Diminishing returns</td>
                </tr>
            </table>
            <p><strong>These are NOT contradictory!</strong> The 6th slice still adds happiness (U↑), just not as much as the 5th (MU↓).</p>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 5. Marginal Rate of Substitution -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">5. Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS)</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <h4>Definition</h4>
            <p>The rate at which a consumer is willing to trade one good for another <strong>while maintaining the same utility level</strong>.</p>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.1rem; text-align: center;">
                    MRS = ΔS/ΔC = −MU<sub>C</sub>/MU<sub>S</sub>
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center;">= Slope of the indifference curve = Ratio of marginal utilities</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Derivation: Why MRS = −MU<sub>C</sub>/MU<sub>S</sub>?</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0; border-left: 4px solid #2563eb;">
                <p><strong>Key idea:</strong> Moving along an IC means utility doesn't change (ΔU = 0)</p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Step 1:</strong> Total change in utility from changing both goods:</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; padding-left: 1rem;">ΔU = (ΔS × MU<sub>S</sub>) + (ΔC × MU<sub>C</sub>)</p>
                <p style="padding-left: 1rem; font-size: 0.9rem; color: #666;">= (change in pizza × utility per pizza) + (change in cookies × utility per cookie)</p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Step 2:</strong> On an IC, utility doesn't change:</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; padding-left: 1rem;">ΔU = 0</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; padding-left: 1rem;">∴ ΔS × MU<sub>S</sub> + ΔC × MU<sub>C</sub> = 0</p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Step 3:</strong> Solve for ΔS/ΔC:</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; padding-left: 1rem;">ΔS × MU<sub>S</sub> = −ΔC × MU<sub>C</sub></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; padding-left: 1rem;">ΔS/ΔC = −MU<sub>C</sub>/MU<sub>S</sub></p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Result:</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; padding-left: 1rem; font-size: 1.1rem;"><strong>MRS = −MU<sub>C</sub>/MU<sub>S</sub></strong></p>
            </div>

            <h4>For U = √(S × C):</h4>
            <div style="background: #faf5ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace;">MRS = −MU<sub>C</sub>/MU<sub>S</sub> = −[S/(2√SC)] / [C/(2√SC)] = <strong>−S/C</strong></p>
            </div>

            <h4>Example: Three Points on an Indifference Curve (U = 2)</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Point</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Pizza (S)</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Cookies (C)</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">MRS = −S/C</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Interpretation</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">A</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">−4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Would give up 4 pizzas for 1 cookie (really wants cookies!)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">B</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">−1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Indifferent: 1 pizza = 1 cookie</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">C</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">−1/4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Would give up only 1/4 pizza for 1 cookie (really wants pizza!)</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>Diminishing MRS: Why ICs are Convex</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0fdf4; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>As you move from A → B → C:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Pizza decreases (4 → 2 → 1) → Pizza becomes more valuable</li>
                    <li>Cookies increase (1 → 2 → 4) → Cookies become less valuable</li>
                    <li>|MRS| decreases (4 → 1 → 0.25) → Less willing to give up pizza for cookies</li>
                </ul>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>This is why indifference curves are convex to the origin!</strong></p>
            </div>

            <h4>DMU vs Diminishing MRS</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Concept</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Meaning</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Relationship</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Diminishing MU</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">More of same good → less extra satisfaction</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Cause</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Diminishing MRS</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">More of one good → less willing to trade the other</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Effect</td>
                </tr>
            </table>
            <p><strong>Connection:</strong> As C↑, MU<sub>C</sub>↓ and MU<sub>S</sub>↑ → MRS = −MU<sub>C</sub>/MU<sub>S</sub> → |MRS|↓</p>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 6. Concave IC -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">6. Why Not Concave Indifference Curves?</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <p>Consider U = S² + C² = 65 (concave to origin)</p>
            
            <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Problem:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>At (8 pizza, 1 cookie): MRS = −1/8 → barely willing to trade pizza for cookies</li>
                    <li>At (4 pizza, 7 cookies): MRS = −7/4 → willing to give up almost 2 pizzas for 1 cookie</li>
                </ul>
                <p><strong>This doesn't make sense!</strong> Why would you give up MORE pizza when you have LESS of it?</p>
            </div>

            <p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> While mathematically valid, concave ICs don't represent typical human preferences. We assume convex ICs (diminishing MRS) as the standard.</p>
        </div>
    </section>
