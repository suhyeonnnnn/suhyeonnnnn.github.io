    <!-- 1. Assumptions on Preferences -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">1. Three Key Assumptions on Preferences</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Assumption</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Meaning</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Implication</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Completeness</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Given two choices, consumers either prefer one, prefer the other, or are indifferent</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">No "I don't know" — consumers always have opinions</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Transitivity</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">If A ≻ B and B ≻ C, then A ≻ C</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Standard mathematical assumption</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Non-satiation</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">More is always better</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">If offered something for free, you'll always take it</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Note on Non-satiation:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Does NOT mean the 10th unit makes you as happy as the 9th</li>
                    <li>Just means the 10th unit is better than not having it</li>
                    <li>Applies to "goods" — things you want (not "bads" like getting poked in the eye)</li>
                </ul>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 2. Indifference Curves -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">2. Indifference Curves</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <p><strong>Definition:</strong> A curve connecting all bundles of goods that give the consumer the same level of satisfaction (utility).</p>

            <h4>Example: Pizza and Cookies</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <ul>
                    <li><strong>Choice A:</strong> 2 slices of pizza, 1 cookie</li>
                    <li><strong>Choice B:</strong> 1 slice of pizza, 2 cookies</li>
                    <li><strong>Choice C:</strong> 2 slices of pizza, 2 cookies</li>
                </ul>
                <p>If indifferent between A and B, but prefer C to both → A and B are on the same indifference curve, C is on a higher one.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Four Properties of Indifference Curves</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">#</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Property</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Reason</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Higher ICs are preferred</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Non-satiation (more is better)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">ICs are downward sloping</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Non-satiation (can't be indifferent between less and more)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">3</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">ICs never cross</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Transitivity + Non-satiation</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Only one IC through any point</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Completeness (must know how you feel)</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>Real-World Example: Job Choice</h4>
            <div style="background: #f5f5f5; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p>A graduate student choosing between jobs based on two dimensions:</p>
                <ul>
                    <li><strong>X-axis:</strong> Weather quality</li>
                    <li><strong>Y-axis:</strong> School/job quality</li>
                </ul>
                <p><strong>Options:</strong> Princeton (great quality, mediocre weather) vs. Santa Cruz (good quality, great weather)</p>
                <p><strong>Decision:</strong> Chose IMF (DC) — better weather than Princeton, better job than Santa Cruz</p>
                <p><em>Note: There's no "right" answer — preferences are personal.</em></p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 3. Utility Functions -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">3. Utility Functions</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <p><strong>Definition:</strong> A mathematical representation of preferences that assigns a number to each bundle of goods.</p>

            <h4>Example: Square Root Utility</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.1rem; text-align: center;">U = √(S × C)</p>
                <p>where S = slices of pizza, C = cookies</p>
            </div>

            <p><strong>Verification:</strong></p>
            <ul>
                <li>U(2, 1) = √2 ≈ 1.41</li>
                <li>U(1, 2) = √2 ≈ 1.41 → Indifferent between A and B ✓</li>
                <li>U(2, 2) = 2 → Prefer C ✓</li>
            </ul>

            <h4>Key Insight: Ordinal, Not Cardinal</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Utility is meaningful only for ranking, not for measuring absolute happiness.</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>"Utility of 3" is meaningless by itself</li>
                    <li>What matters: Is U(A) > U(B) or U(A) < U(B)?</li>
                    <li>U = S × C, U = √(S × C), U = (S × C)² all give the same ranking</li>
                </ul>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 4. Marginal Utility -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">4. Marginal Utility and Diminishing MU</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <h4>Marginal Utility (MU)</h4>
            <p>The additional utility from consuming one more unit of a good.</p>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center;">
                    MUc = ∂U/∂C = 0.5 × S / √(S×C)<br>
                    MUs = ∂U/∂S = 0.5 × C / √(S×C)
                </p>
            </div>

            <h4>Diminishing Marginal Utility ⭐</h4>
            <blockquote style="border-left: 4px solid #2563eb; padding-left: 1rem; margin: 1rem 0; color: #374151;">
                "More is better, but the next unit is not quite as good as the one before."
            </blockquote>

            <div style="background: #f0fdf4; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Why it makes sense:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>The 10th slice of pizza makes you less happy than the 9th</li>
                    <li>Your 5th favorite movie gives less joy than your 4th favorite</li>
                    <li>Key: Utility ↑ with quantity, but Marginal Utility ↓ with quantity</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>Example: Holding Pizza at 2 Slices</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Cookies</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Utility</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Marginal Utility</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">1.41</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">—</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">2.00</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">0.59</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">3</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">2.45</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">0.45</td>
                </tr>
            </table>
            <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280;">Each additional cookie adds less utility → Diminishing MU</p>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 5. Marginal Rate of Substitution -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">5. Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS)</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <h4>Definition</h4>
            <p>The rate at which a consumer is willing to trade one good for another while maintaining the same utility level.</p>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.1rem; text-align: center;">
                    MRS = ΔS/ΔC = −MUc/MUs
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center;">= Slope of the indifference curve = Ratio of marginal utilities</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Derivation</h4>
            <p>Moving along an indifference curve (constant utility):</p>
            <div style="background: #f5f5f5; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center;">
                    ΔS × MUs + ΔC × MUc = 0<br>
                    ∴ MRS = ΔS/ΔC = −MUc/MUs
                </p>
            </div>

            <h4>For U = √(S × C):</h4>
            <div style="background: #faf5ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.1rem; text-align: center;">MRS = −S/C</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Example: Three Points on an Indifference Curve</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Point</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Pizza (S)</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Cookies (C)</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">MRS</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Interpretation</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">A</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">−4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Would give up 4 pizzas for 1 cookie (really wants cookies)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">B</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">−1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Indifferent between pizza and cookies</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">C</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">−1/4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Would give up only 1/4 pizza for 1 cookie (really wants pizza)</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>Diminishing MRS</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0fdf4; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Why indifference curves are convex to the origin:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>As you get more of one good, you're less willing to give up the other for it</li>
                    <li>The more pizza you have, the less you value additional pizza relative to cookies</li>
                    <li>This is the graphical manifestation of diminishing marginal utility</li>
                </ul>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 6. Concave IC -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">6. Why Not Concave Indifference Curves?</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <p>Consider U = S² + C² = 65 (concave to origin)</p>
            
            <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Problem:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>At (8 pizza, 1 cookie): MRS = −1/8 → barely willing to trade pizza for cookies</li>
                    <li>At (4 pizza, 7 cookies): MRS = −7/4 → willing to give up almost 2 pizzas for 1 cookie</li>
                </ul>
                <p><strong>This doesn't make sense!</strong> Why would you give up MORE pizza when you have LESS of it?</p>
            </div>

            <p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> While mathematically valid, concave ICs don't represent typical human preferences. We assume convex ICs (diminishing MRS) as the standard.</p>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 7. Real-World Application -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">7. Real-World Application: Drink Sizes</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <h4>Evidence of Diminishing Marginal Utility</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Company</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Small</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Large</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">Price Difference</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Starbucks (iced coffee)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">$4.55</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">$5.45</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">$0.90 for 2× quantity</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">McDonald's (Coke)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">$2.29</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">$2.99</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: center;">$0.70 for 2× quantity</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Why?</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>The first 16 oz satisfies most of your thirst</li>
                    <li>The additional 16 oz is nice, but not as valuable</li>
                    <li>Companies know consumers won't pay proportionally more for larger sizes</li>
                    <li>Marginal cost to McDonald's: ~$0.03 (small) vs ~$0.04 (large)</li>
                </ul>
                <p><strong>Business implication:</strong> Companies price based on diminishing MRS — proof that they believe preferences are convex!</p>
            </div>
        </div>
