    <!-- 1. Deriving a Demand Curve -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">1. Deriving a Demand Curve</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>Definition</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Demand Curve:</strong> The relationship between the price charged for a good and the consumer's desired quantity of the good.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Setup (Review)</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">Utility Function</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">U = √(S × C)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">Budget Constraint</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Y = P<sub>S</sub> × S + P<sub>C</sub> × C</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">Income (Y)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">$24</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">Price of Pizza (P<sub>S</sub>)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">$4</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">Price of Cookies (P<sub>C</sub>)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">$2 (initial)</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>Step 1: Solve for Optimal Consumption</h4>
            <div style="background: #f5f5f5; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Optimization Condition:</strong> MRS = MRT</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace;">
                    -S/C = -P<sub>C</sub>/P<sub>S</sub> = -2/4 = -1/2<br>
                    → S = C/2 ... (Equation 1)
                </p>
                <p><strong>Budget Constraint:</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace;">
                    24 = 4S + 2C<br>
                    Substitute (1): 24 = 4(C/2) + 2C = 4C<br>
                    → C* = 6, S* = 3
                </p>
            </div>

            <h4>Step 2: Change Price of Cookies</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">P<sub>C</sub></th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">MRT</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Optimization</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Result</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Point</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">$4/3</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">-1/3</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">S = C/3</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">C=9, S=3</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">C</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">$2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">-1/2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">S = C/2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">C=6, S=3</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">A</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">$3</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">-3/4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">S = 3C/4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">C=4, S=3</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">B</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>Step 3: Draw the Demand Curve</h4>
            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
                <img src="/assets/images/mit1401-fig4-1.png" alt="Figure 4-1: Drawing a demand curve" style="max-width: 100%; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;">Figure 4-1: Deriving the Demand Curve from Utility Maximization</p>

            <div style="background: #f0fdf4; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Key Insight:</strong> The demand curve is derived by optimizing utility at different prices and finding which quantity you want at each price.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Student Question: "Why doesn't pizza quantity change?"</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Great point. Someone noticed. That as we varied the price of cookies, the number of slices of pizza did not change. That is not a general law. That is a specific feature of this utility function."</em></p>
                <p>This is called a <strong>flat cross-price consumption curve</strong> — a special property of this particular utility function that makes calculations simpler.</p>

    <!-- 2. Elasticity of Demand -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">2. Elasticity of Demand</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>Definition</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.1rem; text-align: center;">
                    ε = (ΔQ/Q) / (ΔP/P) ≈ (dQ/dP) × (P/Q)
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center;">"How responsive is demand to price changes?"</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Extreme Cases</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Type</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">ε Value</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Curve Shape</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Substitutes</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Example</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Perfectly Inelastic</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">ε = 0</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Vertical</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">None</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Insulin</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Inelastic</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">-1 < ε < 0</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Steep</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Few</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Gas, Water</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Elastic</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">ε < -1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Flat</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Many</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Luxury goods</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Perfectly Elastic</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">ε = -∞</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Horizontal</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Infinite</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">McDonald's vs Burger King</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>Key Principle: Substitutability = Elasticity</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Here's the key feature that differentiates elasticity. It's substitutability. Goods with no substitutes are not elastic. Goods with lots of substitutes are very elastic."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>Classroom Examples</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Student:</strong> "Gas?"</p>
                <p><strong>Gruber:</strong> "Gas would be somewhat inelastic because you got to get to work. On the other hand, there's a bus, there's your bike. You could walk, you could buy a new car. So it's not perfectly inelastic."</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Student:</strong> "Insulin?"</p>
                <p><strong>Gruber:</strong> "Insulin is the classic example we use. There's no substitute. When you're having diabetic trauma for insulin, you will not say, 'it's $1 more, I'm just going to die.'"</p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Key:</strong> Price ratio unchanged → MRS = MRT condition unchanged</p>
                <p>Budget line shifts <strong>parallel</strong> (not pivots)</p>
            </div>

            <h4>The Engel Curve</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Definition:</strong> The relationship between income and quantity demanded</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.1rem; text-align: center; margin-top: 0.5rem;">
                    γ = (ΔQ/Q) / (ΔY/Y) ≈ (dQ/dY) × (Y/Q)
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center;"><strong>Income Elasticity of Demand</strong></p>
            </div>

            <h4>Gamma (γ) vs Epsilon (ε)</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Gamma is much more interesting than epsilon. Epsilon, some negative number, 0 to minus infinity. Gamma can have a much larger range."</em></p>
            </div>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Elasticity</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Range</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Constraint</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">ε (Price)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">0 to -∞</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Always ≤ 0</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">γ (Income)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">-∞ to +∞</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">No constraint</td>
                </tr>
            </table>
        </div>
    </section>

        <h2 class="section-title">5. Mechanics of a Price Change</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>Why This Matters</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"When a price changes, two things happen simultaneously. You may not realize it, but actually when a price changes, two things happen."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>Two Simultaneous Effects</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Effect</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Definition</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Mechanism</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Substitution Effect</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Shift away from relatively expensive good</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Relative prices change</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Income Effect</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">You're effectively richer or poorer</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Real purchasing power changes</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"When a price changes, two things happen. Goods become differentially attractive and you're richer or poorer. And both things drive your response to that price change."</em></p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 6. Decomposing Price Changes -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">6. Decomposing Income and Substitution Effects</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>Example Setup</h4>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>Starting Point (A):</strong> C = 6, S = 3 (on BC1)</li>
                <li><strong>Price Change:</strong> P<sub>C</sub>: $2 → $3</li>
                <li><strong>Ending Point (C):</strong> C = 4, S = 3 (on BC2)</li>
            </ul>

            <h4>Measuring the Substitution Effect</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Definition:</strong> Change in quantity holding utility constant</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center;">ΔQ|<sub>Ū</sub></p>
            </div>

            <p><strong>Graphical Method:</strong> Draw an <strong>imaginary budget constraint BC'</strong> with two features:</p>
            <ol>
                <li><strong>Parallel</strong> to new budget constraint (reflects new price ratio -3/4)</li>
                <li><strong>Tangent</strong> to original indifference curve (keeps utility constant)</li>
            </ol>

            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0; border-left: 4px solid #2563eb;">
                <p><em>"We're showing you the change in quantity you get at a constant utility. How do we keep utility constant? We stay on the same indifference curve."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>Results</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Movement</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Effect</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Cookies</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">A → B</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Substitution Effect</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">6 → 4.89</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">B → C</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Income Effect</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">4.89 → 4</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="background: #f0fdf4;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">A → C</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Total Effect</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">6 → 4</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>Substitution Effect is Always Negative</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Mathematical Proof:</strong></p>
                <ol>
                    <li>At tangency: MU<sub>C</sub>/MU<sub>S</sub> = P<sub>C</sub>/P<sub>S</sub></li>
                    <li>P<sub>C</sub>/P<sub>S</sub> went up (cookies more expensive)</li>
                    <li>MU<sub>C</sub>/MU<sub>S</sub> must go up to maintain equality</li>
                    <li>To raise MU<sub>C</sub> → consume fewer cookies (diminishing MU!)</li>
                    <li>To lower MU<sub>S</sub> → consume more pizza</li>
                </ol>
                <p><strong>Conclusion:</strong> Price↑ → Substitution effect is always negative</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Income Effect Sign Depends on Good Type</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Why are you "effectively poorer"?</strong></p>
                <p><em>"Your income itself hasn't fallen, but your resources have, what you can buy with that income has fallen. You're effectively poor."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Student:</strong> "Your opportunity set has been constricted."</p>
                <p><strong>Gruber:</strong> "Your opportunity set has been constricted."</p>
            </div>

            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Good Type</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">When Income↓</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Income Effect</th>
                </tr>

                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Normal</td>

    <!-- 7. Inferior Good Example -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">7. Inferior Good Example: Steak vs Potatoes</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>Setup</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Figure 4.6 is no longer pizza and cookies. Figure 4.6 is now steak and potatoes. Because pizza and cookies are both normal goods."</em></p>
                <p><em>"Potatoes is the classic inferior good. Poor people, especially back in the day, used to have to eat potatoes because they were cheap to grow and very filling."</em></p>
            </div>

            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">Income</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">$25</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">Price of Steak</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">$5</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">Price of Potatoes</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">$1 → $3</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>Original State (Point A)</h4>
            <p>Steak: 3.5, Potatoes: 7.5</p>

            <h4>Decomposition</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Effect</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Potatoes</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Explanation</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Substitution (A→B)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">7.5 → 4 ↓</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Price↑ → want less (always!)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Income (B→C)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">4 → 5 ↑</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Poorer + Inferior good → want more!</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="background: #fef3c7;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Net Effect</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">7.5 → 5 ↓</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Effects work against each other</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"The income effect goes the other way because potatoes are an inferior good. In other words, now that you're so poor, now potatoes are so expensive, you don't have enough money for steak anymore. So you got to have more potatoes. It's ironic, right?"</em></p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 8. Giffen Goods -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">8. Giffen Goods</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>Definition</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p>A <strong>Giffen good</strong> is an inferior good where the income effect dominates the substitution effect, resulting in an <strong>upward-sloping demand curve</strong>.</p>
                <p style="text-align: center; font-weight: 600; margin-top: 0.5rem;">Price↑ → Demand↑ (violates law of demand!)</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Do They Exist?</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"I like the name Giffen because it rhymes with Griffin. And Griffins are imaginary. Giffen goods are also largely imaginary. It's a great theoretical concept, but they don't really exist."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>The Irish Potato Famine Story</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Claim:</strong> During the potato blight, potato prices rose but per capita potato consumption rose. Therefore, potatoes are a Giffen good!</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Gruber's Rebuttal:</strong></p>
                <p><em>"Here's what they got wrong. Here's where empirical economics is fun. They miss the fact that what other effect did the potato blight have? It chased all the people who could afford it out of Ireland. The only people who were left were super poor."</em></p>
                <p><em>"So yeah, on average, potatoes per person went up. It's a Giffen good. What you're missing is the capitas are different. Each person needs more potatoes. It's just the non-potato eaters left."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>Giffen vs Veblen Goods</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Student:</strong> "A Veblen good?"</p>
                <p><strong>Gruber:</strong> "A Veblen good is a totally different concept. That's the concept that basically you want more when the price is up because you care about your social standing. But that's a behavioral economics thing. We'll come back to that type of behavior. It's not a standard economics effect."</p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>
