    <!-- 1. From Production to Costs -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">1. From Production Function to Cost Function</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>1.1 Why Do We Care About Costs?</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Remember, the way firms maximize profits is by minimizing costs, by producing goods as efficiently as possible at minimum costs."</em></p>
            </div>
            <p>Last lecture, we developed the production function. Now we translate that into costs, because that's what firms actually control.</p>

            <h4>1.2 The Cost Function</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Definition:</strong> The <strong>cost function</strong> C(q) tells us the total cost of producing quantity q.</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; margin-top: 0.5rem; font-size: 1.2rem;">
                    C(q) = r·K̄ + w·L(q)
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem;">
                    where r = rental rate of capital, w = wage, K̄ = fixed capital, L(q) = labor needed for q
                </p>
            </div>

            <h4>1.3 Deriving the Cost Function: A Worked Example</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Given:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Production function: q = √(L × K)</li>
                    <li>Wage: w = 5</li>
                    <li>Rental rate: r = 10</li>
                    <li>Fixed capital: K̄ = 1</li>
                </ul>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Step 1:</strong> Solve production function for L</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">q = √(L × K̄) → q² = L × K̄ → L = q²/K̄</p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Step 2:</strong> Substitute into cost function</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">C(q) = r·K̄ + w·L = 10(1) + 5(q²/1) = 10 + 5q²</p>
                
                <div style="background: #dcfce7; padding: 0.75rem; border-radius: 4px; margin-top: 1rem;">
                    <p style="text-align: center; font-weight: 600;">Final Cost Function: C(q) = 10 + 5q²</p>
                </div>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Where do w and r come from? I'll teach you that in a few lectures. Remember, we peel the onion in this class. We're revealing the mystery slowly. Right now, just take those as given."</em></p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 2. Types of Costs -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">2. Types of Costs</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>2.1 Key Definitions</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Cost Type</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Definition</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">In Our Example</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Formula</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Fixed Costs (FC)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Costs that do NOT vary in the short run</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">10</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">r·K̄</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Variable Costs (VC)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Costs that CAN vary in the short run</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">5q²</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">w·L(q)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="background: #f0fdf4;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Total Costs (TC)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Sum of fixed and variable costs</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">10 + 5q²</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">FC + VC</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>2.2 Marginal Cost: The Key Concept</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Marginal Cost (MC):</strong> The cost of producing <em>one more unit</em></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; margin-top: 0.5rem; font-size: 1.3rem;">
                    MC = dC/dq = dVC/dq
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem;">
                    (In short run, dTC/dq = dVC/dq because fixed costs don't change)
                </p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>In our example:</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">MC = d(10 + 5q²)/dq = <strong>10q</strong></p>
            </div>

            <h4>2.3 Average Costs</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Average Cost Type</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Formula</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">In Our Example</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Average Fixed Cost (AFC)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">FC/q</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">10/q</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Average Variable Cost (AVC)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">VC/q</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">5q</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Average (Total) Cost (AC)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">TC/q = AFC + AVC</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">10/q + 5q</td>
                </tr>
            </table>
        </div>
    </section>

            <h4>3.1 Understanding the Shapes</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Curve</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Shape</th>

                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">MC = 10q</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Upward sloping (linear)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Each additional unit costs more (diminishing returns to labor)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">AFC = 10/q</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Always falling (hyperbola)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Fixed costs spread over more units</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">AVC = 5q</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Always rising (linear)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Diminishing marginal product of labor</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">AC = 10/q + 5q</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">U-shaped (first falls, then rises)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">AFC pulls it down at first, then AVC pulls it up</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>3.2 Why Average Cost is U-Shaped</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Average costs first fall, then rise. Why is that? Well, that's because at first, you have these big fixed costs you have to pay off. But over time, the fixed costs get smaller relative to the variable costs."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>3.3 Critical Relationship: MC Intersects AC at Minimum</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Key Rule:</strong> Marginal Cost crosses Average Cost at the minimum of Average Cost.</p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Intuition:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>If MC < AC: Producing one more unit brings down the average → AC is falling</li>
                    <li>If MC > AC: Producing one more unit brings up the average → AC is rising</li>
                    <li>At MC = AC: Average is neither rising nor falling → minimum!</li>
                </ul>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"Wherever the marginal cost is above the average cost, the average cost is rising. Wherever the marginal cost is below the average cost, it's falling. Therefore, marginal cost intersects average cost at the minimum."</em></p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 4. MC and MPL Relationship -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">4. The Link Between Marginal Cost and Marginal Product</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>4.1 Deriving the Relationship</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Starting from:</strong> C = r·K̄ + w·L</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Differentiate with respect to q:</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">MC = dC/dq = w · (dL/dq)</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>But recall:</strong> MP<sub>L</sub> = dq/dL, so dL/dq = 1/MP<sub>L</sub></p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.3rem;">
                    MC = w / MP<sub>L</sub>
                </p>
            </div>

            <h4>4.2 Interpretation</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"The marginal cost of producing the next unit of the good is higher the more you have to pay each worker and lower the more productive each worker is."</em></p>
            </div>

            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">If...</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Then MC...</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Intuition</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">w ↑ (wage rises)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; color: #dc2626; font-weight: 600;">MC ↑</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">More expensive labor → higher cost per unit</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">MP<sub>L</sub> ↑ (productivity rises)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; color: #16a34a; font-weight: 600;">MC ↓</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Each worker produces more → need fewer workers per unit</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">MP<sub>L</sub> ↓ (diminishing returns)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; color: #dc2626; font-weight: 600;">MC ↑</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">This is why MC curves slope upward!</td>
                </tr>
            </table>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 5. Sunk Costs -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">5. Sunk Costs: A Critical Distinction</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>5.1 Fixed vs. Sunk Costs</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Last lecture, I said in the long run, all inputs are variable. I'm going to already contradict that. We have some costs which in the long run are not variable. Those are called sunk costs."</em></p>
            </div>

            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Cost Type</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Definition</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Can You Recover It?</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Example</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Fixed Cost</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Fixed in short run, variable in long run</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; color: #16a34a; font-weight: 600;">Yes (sell it)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Machine, building</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="background: #fef2f2;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Sunk Cost</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">NEVER recoverable, even in long run</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; color: #dc2626; font-weight: 600;">No (gone forever)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Med school, advertising</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"A sunk cost is something you can't ever get rid of or sell. The best example would be med school. If you want to be a doctor, you have to go to med school. Having gone to med school, you can't ever un-go to med school. You have paid that cost of time and money, and you're done. It's sunk."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>5.2 The Sunk Cost Fallacy</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p style="font-size: 1.1rem; font-weight: 600; text-align: center; color: #dc2626;">
                    "Sunk costs are irrelevant to decision making once they're paid."
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"Or as the economists say, sunk costs are sunk. Doesn't matter. It's irrelevant."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>5.3 The Journey Concert Example</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Gruber's Story:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Bought Journey concert tickets for $125 each ($250 total)</li>
                    <li>Looked at setlist, realized he only likes 3 songs</li>
                    <li>Decided to sell tickets on StubHub</li>
                </ul>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Question:</strong> How should the $250 he paid affect the price he sets on StubHub?</p>
                
                <div style="background: #dcfce7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin-top: 1rem;">
                    <p style="font-weight: 600;">Answer: It shouldn't! It's a sunk cost.</p>
                    <p><em>"I paid it already. It's irrelevant. It's done. I already paid the 250. It's gone. I might feel sad, but it's gone."</em></p>
                </div>
            </div>

            <h4>5.4 What SHOULD Determine the Price?</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>The opportunity cost of going!</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>If you literally don't want to go → willing to sell for $0</li>
                    <li>If you'd pay $50 to go → set minimum price at $50</li>
                    <li>If someone pays more than your value → sell</li>
                    <li>If someone pays less than your value → keep tickets and go</li>
                </ul>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"The amount I should be willing to get should be determined by the opportunity cost of going, not by what I paid for them."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>5.5 Student Q&A: "But I Want to Recover My Sunk Costs!"</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Student:</strong> "I feel like I would think about trying to recover those sunk costs."</p>
                <p><strong>Gruber:</strong> <em>"Yeah, you would because that's the way humans think. But it's wrong."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"Let me put it this way. Let's say I could go online and sell them for $500 each. Should I only sell them for $250 because that's what I paid for them? No, sell them for $500. So it's irrelevant that I paid $250."</em></p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 6. Long-Run vs Short-Run Costs -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">6. Long-Run vs. Short-Run Costs</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>6.1 The Key Intuition</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-size: 1.1rem; font-weight: 600; text-align: center;">
                    Long-run costs ≤ Short-run costs (always!)
                </p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Long-run costs will always be at the lower bound of short-run costs. The cost of production in the long run are always the same or lower than the cost of production in the short run."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Why?</strong> <em>"The more variables you have to toy with, the more you can optimize."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>6.2 LRAC as Envelope of SRAC Curves</h4>
            
            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
                <img src="/assets/images/fig6-2-lrac-envelope.png" alt="Figure 6-2: LRAC as Envelope of SRAC" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Figure 6-2:</strong> Long-Run Average Cost (LRAC) as the envelope of Short-Run Average Cost curves.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>6.3 Understanding the Graph</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Think of this as three different plant sizes (different K̄ choices):</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li><strong>SRAC¹:</strong> Small plant (low K̄) - efficient at low output q₁</li>
                    <li><strong>SRAC²:</strong> Medium plant - efficient at medium output q₂</li>
                    <li><strong>SRAC³:</strong> Large plant (high K̄) - efficient at high output q₃</li>
                </ul>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"If the firm wants to produce q₁, the most efficient K̄ is the one that yields SRAC¹. If the firm wants to produce q₃, the most efficient K̄ is the one that yields SRAC³."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>6.4 Real-World Example: Tesla</h4>

                <p><strong>The Tesla Battery Factory Story:</strong></p>

    <!-- 7. Cost Minimization in the Long Run -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">7. Cost Minimization in the Long Run</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>7.1 The Isocost Line</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Isocost Line:</strong> All combinations of L and K that cost the same total amount.</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; margin-top: 0.5rem; font-size: 1.2rem;">
                    C = w·L + r·K
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280;">Rearranging: K = C/r - (w/r)·L</p>
            </div>

            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
                <img src="/assets/images/fig6-3-isocost-lines.png" alt="Figure 6-3: Isocost Lines" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Figure 6-3:</strong> Isocost lines for w=$5, r=$10. Each line shows combinations of L and K that cost the same.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>7.2 Properties of Isocost Lines</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Property</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Value</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Interpretation</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Slope</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">-w/r = -5/10 = -1/2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Give up 1/2 unit of K to get 1 unit of L (at same cost)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">L-intercept</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">C/w</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Maximum L if spend all on labor</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">K-intercept</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">C/r</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Maximum K if spend all on capital</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>7.3 The Cost Minimization Problem</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Goal:</strong> For any given output q, find the combination of L and K that minimizes cost.</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Solution:</strong> Find the tangency between the isoquant (from production function) and the isocost line.</p>
            </div>

            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
                <img src="/assets/images/fig6-4-cost-minimization.png" alt="Figure 6-4: Cost Minimization" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Figure 6-4:</strong> Cost minimization at tangency. To produce √12.5 units at minimum cost ($50), use L=5 and K=2.5.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>7.4 The Tangency Condition</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>At the optimum:</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.2rem;">
                    Slope of Isoquant = Slope of Isocost
                </p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.2rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;">
                    MRTS = -MP<sub>L</sub>/MP<sub>K</sub> = -w/r
                </p>
            </div>

            <h4>7.5 Why Producer Theory is Harder than Consumer Theory</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"This is why producer theory is harder. Because I didn't tell you the right isocost. With consumer theory, I gave you a budget constraint. I'm not giving you an isocost here. I'm just saying there are multiple possible isocosts."</em></p>
                
                <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                    <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Consumer Theory</th>
                        <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Producer Theory</th>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Budget is fixed (given income)</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Budget is a choice (how big to be)</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">2 equations, 2 unknowns → solution</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">2 equations, 2 unknowns → formula only</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Done at tangency!</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Need to choose q first!</td>
                    </tr>
                </table>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 8. Long-Run Expansion Path -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">8. The Long-Run Expansion Path</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>8.1 Definition</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Long-Run Expansion Path:</strong> The set of cost-minimizing combinations of L and K for every possible output level q.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>8.2 Linear Expansion Path</h4>
            
            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">

            </div>

    <!-- 9. Accounting vs Economic Costs -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">9. Measuring Costs: Accounting vs. Economics</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>9.1 The Key Difference</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"This is where I get to tell you that economics is just cooler than accounting. And let me explain why. Accounting only considers the cash-flow costs that you have when you start a firm. Economics more appropriately considers opportunity costs, as well."</em></p>
            </div>

            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Type</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Definition</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Includes</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Accounting Costs</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">What you actually spend (cash outflows)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Wages, rent, materials, etc.</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="background: #f0fdf4;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Economic Costs</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Opportunity costs of ALL resources</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Accounting costs + foregone alternatives</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>9.2 The Web Design Startup Example</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Scenario:</strong> You (MIT graduate) start a web design firm after graduation.</p>
                <ul>
                    <li>You work full time (no salary to yourself)</li>
                    <li>Hire one programmer for $40,000/year</li>
                    <li>Give programmer your extra computer (1 year left before it's useless)</li>
                    <li>After 1 year, revenue = $60,000</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">

                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"Remember, that's why we call economics the dismal science. We point out nothing is free. Your time wasn't free. The time you dedicate to this firm meant you could have been doing something else. And that's not free."</em></p>
