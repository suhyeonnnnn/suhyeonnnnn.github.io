    <!-- 2. Perfect Competition -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">2. Perfect Competition: Definition and Assumptions</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>2.1 The Key Definition</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Price-Takers:</strong> In a perfectly competitive market, all firms are <strong>price-takers</strong>. No action by any individual firm can affect the market price.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>2.2 When Does This Hold? Perfectly Elastic Firm Demand</h4>
            
            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
                <img src="/assets/images/fig7-1-perfectly-elastic-demand.png" alt="Figure 7-1: Perfectly Elastic Demand" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Figure 7-1:</strong> In perfect competition, each firm faces perfectly elastic demand. Note: x-axis is <strong>little q</strong> (firm), not big Q (market).</p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"This does not say the market demand is perfectly elastic. The demand for any given firm is very elastic. I'm not saying the demand for Eiffel Tower statuettes in general is perfectly elastic. I'm saying that across sellers, the firm-specific demand is perfectly elastic."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>2.3 Three Key Assumptions</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">#</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Assumption</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Meaning</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Identical Products</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">All firms sell the exact same thing</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">2</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Full Price Knowledge</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Every consumer knows every firm's price</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">3</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">No Transaction Costs</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">You can easily shop at any firm</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>2.4 Real-World Examples</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>eBay (Almost Perfect):</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Products are (somewhat) identical</li>

    <!-- 3. Profit Maximization -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">3. Profit Maximization in Perfect Competition</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>3.1 The Firm's Objective</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.2rem;">
                    max π(q) = R(q) - C(q)
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280;">where R(q) = revenue, C(q) = cost</p>
            </div>

            <h4>3.2 The First-Order Condition</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Take derivative and set to zero:</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">dπ/dq = dR/dq - dC/dq = 0</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>MR = MC</strong></p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #dcfce7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p style="font-size: 1.1rem; font-weight: 600; text-align: center;">
                    General Rule: Marginal Revenue = Marginal Cost
                </p>
            </div>

            <h4>3.3 In Perfect Competition: MR = P</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"In a competitive market, we know what the marginal revenue is. What do you get from selling the next unit? Well, you get the market price, p. It's given to you. The marginal revenue is always p."</em></p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-size: 1.3rem; font-weight: 600; text-align: center; color: #2563eb;">
                    P = MC
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280;">The profit maximization rule in perfect competition</p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 4. Worked Example -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">4. Worked Example: Finding Optimal Output and Profits</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>4.1 Setup</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <ul>
                    <li>Cost function: <strong>C(q) = 10 + 5q²</strong></li>
                    <li>Market price: <strong>p = 30</strong></li>
                    <li>Marginal cost: <strong>MC = dC/dq = 10q</strong></li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>4.2 Finding Optimal Quantity</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Set P = MC:</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">30 = 10q</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;"><strong>q* = 3</strong></p>
            </div>

            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
                <img src="/assets/images/fig7-2-profit-maximization.png" alt="Figure 7-2: Profit Maximization" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Figure 7-2:</strong> Left: Revenue (R) and Cost (C) curves. Right: Profit curve showing maximum at q = 3.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>4.3 The Hill-Climbing Intuition</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Think of yourself as climbing a mountain that's in the clouds. And you're trying to get to the top of the mountain, but you can't see more than one step in front of you. If you take a step forward and you go up, you know you're heading up the mountain. Take a step forward and you go down, you're heading down the mountain."</em></p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Marginal Decision-Making:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>At q = 1, 2, 3: MR > MC → each unit adds to profit → keep going up!</li>
                    <li>At q = 4, 5, 6: MR < MC → each unit reduces profit → going down!</li>
                    <li>At q = 3: MR = MC → at the top of the hill</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>4.4 Calculating Profits</h4>
            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
                <img src="/assets/images/fig7-3-cost-curves-profit.png" alt="Figure 7-3: Profit Rectangle" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Figure 7-3:</strong> Profit = (P - ATC) × q = blue rectangle.</p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Step 1: Find Average Cost at q = 3</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">AC = C(q)/q = (10 + 5q²)/q = 10/q + 5q</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">AC(3) = 10/3 + 5(3) = 3.33 + 15 = <strong>18.33</strong></p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Step 2: Calculate Profit per Unit</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">Profit per unit = P - AC = 30 - 18.33 = <strong>11.67</strong></p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Step 3: Calculate Total Profit</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">π = (P - AC) × q = 11.67 × 3 ≈ <strong>35</strong></p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #dcfce7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p style="text-align: center;"><strong>Profit = (Price - Average Cost) × Quantity</strong></p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280;">This is the area of the blue rectangle in Figure 7-3</p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 6. The Shutdown Rule -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">6. The Shutdown Rule: When to Stop Producing</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>6.1 Key Insight: Losing Money ≠ Shutdown</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"In the short run, losing money is not necessarily a reason to shut down. You can lose money and want to stay in business."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>6.2 Example: Price Falls to $10</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>With C(q) = 10 + 5q² and p = 10:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>MC = 10q, so at p = MC: q* = 1</li>
                    <li>Revenue = 10 × 1 = 10</li>
                    <li>Cost = 10 + 5(1)² = 15</li>
                    <li>Profit = 10 - 15 = <strong>-5 (losing money!)</strong></li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>6.3 Should You Shut Down?</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>If you produce q = 1:</strong> Profit = -5</p>
                <p><strong>If you produce q = 0:</strong> Profit = -10 (still pay fixed costs!)</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem; color: #16a34a; font-weight: 600;">Better to produce and lose 5 than shut down and lose 10!</p>
            </div>

            <h4>6.4 The Shutdown Rule</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.2rem;">
                    Shut down when: P < AVC
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280;">Keep producing as long as price covers variable costs</p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Fixed costs are, in the short run, sunk. They're irrelevant. You already paid them. You just want to ask for the next unit: Will I make money?"</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>6.5 In Our Example: Never Shut Down</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>For C(q) = 10 + 5q²:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>VC = 5q²</li>
                    <li>AVC = 5q</li>
                    <li>At optimum (MC = P): 10q = P → q = P/10</li>
                    <li>So AVC = 5(P/10) = 0.5P</li>
                </ul>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem; color: #16a34a; font-weight: 600;">Since AVC = 0.5P < P always, the firm never shuts down with this cost function.</p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

        <h2 class="section-title">7. Deriving the Supply Curve</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>7.1 The Firm's Supply Decision</h4>
            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
                <img src="/assets/images/fig7-5-firm-supply-decision.png" alt="Figure 7-5: Firm Supply Decision" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Figure 7-5:</strong> At each price, the firm produces where P = MC.</p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <ul>
                    <li>At P = 10: produce q = 1</li>
                    <li>At P = 20: produce q = 2</li>
                    <li>At P = 30: produce q = 3</li>
                    <li>At P = 40: produce q = 4</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>7.2 The Key Insight</h4>
            <div style="background: #dcfce7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p style="font-size: 1.1rem; font-weight: 600; text-align: center;">
                    The Supply Curve IS the Marginal Cost Curve
                </p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"The demand curve was the marginal willingness to pay for a good. The supply curve is the marginal cost of producing the next good."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>7.3 The Short-Run Firm Supply Curve</h4>
            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">

                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Figure 7-6:</strong> Short-run firm supply curve S = MC = 10q.</p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Technical Definition:</strong></p>
                <p style="text-align: center;">Short-run supply curve = MC curve <strong>above the shutdown point</strong></p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280;">(above minimum AVC)</p>
            </div>

            <h4>7.4 Long-Run vs. Short-Run Supply</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;"></th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Short Run</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Long Run</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Supply Curve</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">MC above min AVC</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">MC (full stop)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Shutdown Rule</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">P < AVC</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">π < 0</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Why?</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Can re-optimize K later</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Already optimized everything</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"In the long run, if you've done all your optimizing and you're still losing money, you're just shit out of luck. You should get out of the business because there's no more optimization you can do."</em></p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 8. From Firm to Market Supply -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">8. From Firm Supply to Market Supply</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
                <img src="/assets/images/fig7-7-sr-market-supply.png" alt="Figure 7-7: Market Supply" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Figure 7-7:</strong> Adding identical firms makes market supply more elastic. S₁ = 1 firm, S₂ = 2 firms, S₃ = 3 firms.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>8.1 Aggregating Firm Supply</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>With n identical firms:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Each firm: q = P/10</li>
                    <li>Market: Q = n × (P/10)</li>
                </ul>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Example with n = 1:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>At P = 10: Q = 1</li>
                    <li>At P = 30: Q = 3</li>
                </ul>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Example with n = 2:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>At P = 10: Q = 2</li>
                    <li>At P = 30: Q = 6</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>8.2 Key Insight: More Firms = More Elastic Supply</h4>
            <div style="background: #dcfce7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p style="font-size: 1.1rem; font-weight: 600; text-align: center;">
                    More identical firms → Flatter (more elastic) market supply curve
                </p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"If I said competitive markets have an infinite number of firms and the more firms there are, the more elastic the supply curve, think about what that implies to the supply curve..."</em></p>
                <p style="color: #6b7280; margin-top: 0.5rem;">(We'll explore this next lecture!)</p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 9. Full Equilibrium Derivation -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">9. Putting It All Together: Full Equilibrium</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"This is the beauty of being at MIT. You'll never find an introductory economics course that does this. Before Paul Samuelson taught this class, people would teach the graph and be done with it. You guys want the math? I'm giving you the math."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>Step A: Get the Supply Curve</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>1. Start with production function:</strong> q = √(L × K)</p>
                <p><strong>2. Given:</strong> w = 5, r = 10, K̄ = 1</p>
                <p><strong>3. Derive cost function:</strong> C(q) = 10 + 5q²</p>
                <p><strong>4. Get MC:</strong> MC = 10q</p>
                <p><strong>5. Firm supply (P = MC):</strong> q = P/10</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Step B: Make Market Supply Curve</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Assume n = 6 firms</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">Q = 6 × (P/10) = 3P/5</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Market supply:</strong> Q = (3/5)P</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Step C: Get Demand Curve</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>From consumer theory:</strong> Q = 48 - P</p>
            </div>

            <h4>Step D: Find Equilibrium</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Set supply = demand:</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">(3/5)P = 48 - P</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">(3/5)P + P = 48</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;">(8/5)P = 48</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;"><strong>P* = 30</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin-left: 1rem;"><strong>Q* = 18</strong></p>
            </div>

            <h4>Step E: Verify Individual Firm Behavior</h4>
            <div style="background: #dcfce7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <ul>
                    <li>Market quantity Q* = 18</li>
                    <li>With 6 identical firms: each produces q* = 18/6 = <strong>3</strong></li>
                    <li>Check: At P = 30, firm sets MC = P → 10q = 30 → q = 3 ✓</li>
                </ul>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600; text-align: center;">The magic of the market: Everyone is satisfied!</p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>
