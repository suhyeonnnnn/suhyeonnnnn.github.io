        <h2 class="section-title">1. What Do Firms Want?</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>1.1 The Parallel Between Consumers and Firms</h4>
            <p>Just as we asked "What do consumers want?" (maximize utility given budget constraint), we now ask "What do firms want?"</p>
            
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Agent</th>

            <h4>1.2 What Determines Firm Profits?</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="text-align: center; font-size: 1.1rem;">
                    <strong>Profits (π)</strong> = Revenue - Costs = P × Q - C(Q)
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem;">Profits depend on three things:</p>
            </div>

            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Factor</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Firm Control?</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Notes</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">1. Consumer Demand</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Limited</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Advertising can affect demand, but largely given by market conditions</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">2. Competition</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Limited</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Monopolies can affect market structure, but in standard competitive model, taken as given</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="background: #f0fdf4;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">3. Costs of Production</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Yes!</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">This is what the firm controls—how efficiently it produces</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Key Insight:</strong> <em>"From the firm's perspective, maximizing profits is the same as minimizing costs. The way to maximize profits is to minimize costs. And that's how the firm produces as efficiently as possible, by minimizing costs."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>1.3 Why Focus on Cost Minimization?</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Mathematical Intuition:</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin: 0.5rem 0;">π = P × Q - C(Q)</p>
                <p>If P and Q are determined by market forces (demand and competition), then:</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; margin: 0.5rem 0;">max π ⟺ min C(Q) for any given Q</p>
                <p>In the real world, there are complications, but in our model—and in most of the real world—we say firms' goal is to minimize costs.</p>
            </div>
        </div>

    <!-- 2. Production Functions -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">2. Production Functions</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>2.1 The Firm as a Black Box</h4>
            <p>To understand how to minimize costs, we need to understand where a firm's costs come from. To understand that, we need to understand what firms actually do.</p>
            
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1.5rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0; text-align: center;">
                <div style="display: flex; justify-content: center; align-items: center; gap: 1rem; flex-wrap: wrap;">
                    <div style="background: #dbeafe; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px;">
                        <strong>Inputs</strong><br>
                        <span style="font-size: 0.9rem; color: #6b7280;">Labor (L)<br>Capital (K)</span>
                    </div>
                    <div style="font-size: 2rem;">→</div>
                    <div style="background: #2563eb; color: white; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px;">
                        <strong>Production Function</strong><br>
                        <span style="font-size: 0.9rem;">q = f(L, K)</span>
                    </div>
                    <div style="font-size: 2rem;">→</div>
                    <div style="background: #dcfce7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px;">
                        <strong>Output</strong><br>
                        <span style="font-size: 0.9rem; color: #6b7280;">Goods & Services (q)</span>
                    </div>
                </div>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"In this course, firms are simply black boxes for much of the course. They're simply boxes where inputs go in and outputs come out. And in the middle is something called a production function. That production function is the magic that makes—is the magic of the firm."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>2.2 Formal Definition</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Production Function:</strong> A mathematical description of what is <em>technically feasible</em> for the firm to produce given its inputs.</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; margin-top: 0.5rem; font-size: 1.2rem;">
                    q = f(L, K)
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem;">where q = output, L = labor, K = capital</p>
            </div>

            <h4>2.3 Comparing Utility and Production Functions</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Aspect</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Utility Function</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Production Function</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">What it represents</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">How goods → happiness</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">How inputs → output</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Tangibility</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Abstract (can't measure happiness)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Concrete (can measure output)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Example</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">U = √(cookies × pizza)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">q = √(L × K)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Gruber's quote</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">"A vague thing"</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">"The technology"</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Just like the utility function is a mathematical representation of how goods turn into happiness, the production function, which, in some sense, is easier to understand, is a mathematical representation of how inputs turn to outputs."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>2.4 Notation: Little q vs Big Q</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600; font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.1rem;">q</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">(lowercase)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Output of a <strong>specific firm</strong></td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; color: #6b7280;">e.g., "How many cars does Ford produce?"</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600; font-family: monospace; font-size: 1.1rem;">Q</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">(uppercase)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Total <strong>market</strong> output</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; color: #6b7280;">e.g., "How many cars are produced in total?"</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>2.5 Student Q&A: "Shouldn't q just be L plus K?"</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Student Question:</strong> "Why isn't the production function just q = L + K?"</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Gruber's Answer:</strong></p>
                <p><em>"No. q could be L plus K. That would be a particular form of f. But just like you can't tell me what form my utility function is, I can't tell you what form your production function is. This is some rich function of how you translate L and K to q."</em></p>
                
                <div style="background: white; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin-top: 1rem;">
                    <p><strong>Examples of different production functions:</strong></p>
                    <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                        <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                            <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-family: monospace;">q = L + K</td>
                            <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">1 worker = 1 machine (perfect substitutes)</td>
                        </tr>
                        <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                            <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-family: monospace;">q = L + 5K</td>
                            <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">1 machine = 5 workers</td>
                        </tr>
                        <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                            <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-family: monospace;">q = √(L × K)</td>
                            <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Diminishing returns, some substitutability</td>
                        </tr>
                        <tr>
                            <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-family: monospace;">q = min(L, K)</td>
                            <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Must use together (perfect complements)</td>
                        </tr>
                    </table>
                </div>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"So just like utility isn't just cookies plus slices, it's some function of cookies and slices, production is some function of labor and capital."</em></p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

            <h4>3.2 The "Rental" Intuition</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Key Idea:</strong> Think of ALL inputs as being <em>rented</em>.</p>
                <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Wage (w)</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">= Price you pay to "rent" an hour of a worker's time</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Rental Rate (r)</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">= Price you pay to "rent" a unit of capital (machine, building)</td>
                    </tr>
                </table>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"In other words, think of whatever you use in production as rented. You rent a machine. You rent a building. In some sense, workers, the wage is the price you pay to rent an hour of a worker's time."</em></p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Why this framing?</strong></p>
                <p><em>"This makes it easy and allows for a parallel analysis between workers and machines. They're both things you rent."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;">This allows us to write the <strong>cost function</strong> simply as:</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.1rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;">
                    C = wL + rK
                </p>
            </div>

            <h4>3.3 Where Do Prices Come From?</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Where does the wage come from? We'll discuss that in a few lectures when we cover labor markets."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;">For now, we take w and r as <strong>given</strong> (exogenous) and focus on how firms choose L and K.</p>
            </div>
        </div>

    <!-- 5. Short-Run Production -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">5. Short-Run Production</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>5.1 The Short-Run Production Function</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.3rem;">
                    q = f(L, K̄)
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280;">Capital is fixed by definition in the short-run</p>
            </div>

            <p>The firm's question: <em>"Look, I can't change the machines I have, that's fixed. In the future I could change it, but for my decision today—I've gone to work today, September 25, I showed up at work. I have a decision to make today, which is how many workers do I want today?"</em></p>

            <h4>5.2 Marginal Product of Labor (MP<sub>L</sub>)</h4>
            <div style="background: #f5f5f5; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Definition:</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.2rem;">
                    MP<sub>L</sub> = ∂q/∂L = ∂f(L, K̄)/∂L
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center;"><em>"How valuable is the next worker in terms of delivering output?"</em></p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Remember:</strong> This course always works on <strong>marginal</strong> decisions!</p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Not "should I hire workers?" but "should I hire <em>one more</em> worker?"</li>
                    <li>Not "is labor valuable?" but "how valuable is the <em>next</em> unit of labor?"</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>5.3 The Law of Diminishing Marginal Product</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Key Assumption:</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.2rem;">
                    ∂²q/∂L² < 0
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center;">The <em>second</em> derivative is negative</p>
            </div>

            <h4>5.4 Critical Clarification (Student Q&A)</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Student Question:</strong> "Does diminishing marginal product mean MP<sub>L</sub> is negative?"</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Gruber's Answer:</strong> <em>"No! This is diminishing, not negative!"</em></p>
                
                <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0; background: white;">
                    <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Derivative</th>
                        <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Sign</th>
                        <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Meaning</th>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">∂q/∂L (MP<sub>L</sub>)</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; color: #16a34a; font-weight: 600;">> 0 (Positive)</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Each additional worker adds value</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">∂²q/∂L²</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; color: #dc2626; font-weight: 600;">< 0 (Negative)</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Each additional worker adds <em>less and less</em></td>
                    </tr>
                </table>
                
                <p><em>"This does not mean diminishing total product. Once again, like with consumers, more is generally better, more workers are better. But each worker, given fixed capital, adds less to output."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>5.5 Graphical Representation</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Total Product Curve (q vs L):</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Starts at origin (0 workers = 0 output)</li>
                    <li>Increases as L increases (more workers = more output)</li>
                    <li>Curve is <strong>concave</strong> (bends downward) due to diminishing MP<sub>L</sub></li>
                </ul>
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Marginal Product Curve (MP<sub>L</sub> vs L):</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Positive (above x-axis)</li>
                    <li>Downward sloping (diminishing)</li>
                    <li>MP<sub>L</sub> = slope of Total Product curve at each point</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>5.7 The Intuition Behind Diminishing MP<sub>L</sub></h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"The idea of diminishing marginal product is: given that you only have one shovel, given your fixed capital, each additional worker does less and less good."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"It's not quite as simple an intuition as diminishing marginal utility, but I think it's sort of natural."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Exception:</strong> <em>"And it's not always true. Like I said, you can imagine the second worker might be as good as the first worker. But in general, each additional worker will add less and less value if they're working with the same number of machines."</em></p>
            </div>

            <p><em>"In the long-run, you can take more advantage. You could add wheelbarrows and more shovels. And you could add lots of capital and then make each worker more productive. But in the short-run, with only one piece of capital, that one shovel, there is nothing else you can do."</em></p>

        <h2 class="section-title">6. Long-Run Production</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>6.1 Why the Long-Run is More Interesting</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"The long-run is more interesting because now, you can vary both capital and labor. So now, the problem is going to look just like the consumer choice problem."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Parallel Structure:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Consumer: Choose between cookies and pizza</li>
                    <li>Producer: Choose between labor and capital</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>6.2 Example Production Function</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.3rem;">
                    q = √(L × K) = L<sup>0.5</sup> × K<sup>0.5</sup>
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280;">A Cobb-Douglas production function—familiar from consumer theory!</p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Example Calculations:</strong></p>
                <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">L = 4, K = 4</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">→ q = √(4×4) = √16 = 4</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">L = 1, K = 16</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">→ q = √(1×16) = √16 = 4</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">L = 16, K = 1</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">→ q = √(16×1) = √16 = 4</td>
                    </tr>
                </table>
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem;">Notice: Same output can be achieved with different combinations!</p>
            </div>

            <h4>6.3 Isoquants: The Producer's "Indifference Curves"</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Definition:</strong> An <strong>isoquant</strong> is the set of all combinations of K and L that deliver the <em>same</em> amount of output q.</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"This illustrates a set of combinations of K and L which deliver the same amount of q. Look familiar? It should look familiar. These look like indifference curves. But in this context, we call them isoquants, because we like making up cool words."</em></p>
            </div>

            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
                <img src="/assets/images/fig5-1-isoquants.png" alt="Figure 5-1: Isoquants" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Figure 5-1:</strong> Isoquants for q = √(K·L). Each curve represents a different output level.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>6.4 Isoquant Map (Graphical Representation)</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Picture:</strong> Graph with K on vertical axis, L on horizontal axis</p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Each curve represents a different output level (q = 1, q = 2, q = 3, ...)</li>
                    <li>Curves further from origin = higher output</li>
                    <li>Along each curve, output is constant</li>
                </ul>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>For q = √(L×K), the isoquant for q = 4:</strong></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace;">4 = √(L×K) → 16 = L×K → K = 16/L</p>
                <p>This is a <strong>rectangular hyperbola</strong>.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>6.5 Properties of Isoquants (Same as Indifference Curves!)</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Property</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Meaning</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Why?</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">1. Further out is better</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Higher isoquants = more output</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">More inputs → more output</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">2. Slope downward</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Trade-off between L and K</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">To keep q constant, ↑L requires ↓K</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">3. Don't cross</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Same logic as indifference curves</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">If they crossed, same inputs would give different outputs</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">4. Convex to origin</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Diminishing MRTS</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Diminishing marginal products</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>6.6 What Determines the Shape of Isoquants?</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0fdf4; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Student Answer:</strong> <em>"Depending on how the different inputs go with each other?"</em></p>
                <p><strong>Gruber:</strong> <em>"Exactly. It's going to depend on the substitutability of the different inputs. The more substitutable, the straighter is the isoquant; the less substitutable, the more bent is the isoquant."</em></p>
            </div>

    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">7. Marginal Rate of Technical Substitution (MRTS)</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Terminology Warning:</strong> <em>"Now with consumers, you had marginal rate of substitution and marginal rate of transformation, MRS and MRT. Now I'm going to have MRTS, which is the marginal rate of technical substitution—just to really mess you up."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>7.1 Definition</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>MRTS:</strong> The rate at which you can trade off labor and capital while producing the <em>same</em> quantity of output.</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; margin-top: 0.5rem; font-size: 1.2rem;">
                    MRTS = dK/dL|<sub>q constant</sub> = Slope of isoquant
                </p>
            </div>

            <h4>7.2 Mathematical Derivation</h4>
            <div style="background: #f5f5f5; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Question:</strong> What happens with a marginal increase in L and marginal decrease in K, staying on the same isoquant?</p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 1rem;"><strong>Step 1:</strong> Along an isoquant, output is constant (dq = 0)</p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Step 2:</strong> Total differential of production function:</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center;">dq = (∂q/∂L)dL + (∂q/∂K)dK = MP<sub>L</sub>·dL + MP<sub>K</sub>·dK</p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Step 3:</strong> Set dq = 0 (staying on isoquant):</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center;">0 = MP<sub>L</sub>·dL + MP<sub>K</sub>·dK</p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Step 4:</strong> Solve for dK/dL:</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center;">MP<sub>K</sub>·dK = -MP<sub>L</sub>·dL</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.1rem; background: #eff6ff; padding: 0.5rem; border-radius: 4px;">
                    dK/dL = -MP<sub>L</sub>/MP<sub>K</sub> = MRTS
                </p>
            </div>

            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Compare to Consumer Theory:</strong></p>
                <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">MRS (Consumer)</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-family: monospace;">= -MU<sub>x</sub>/MU<sub>y</sub></td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Slope of indifference curve</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-weight: 600;">MRTS (Producer)</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem; font-family: monospace;">= -MP<sub>L</sub>/MP<sub>K</sub></td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.5rem;">Slope of isoquant</td>
                    </tr>
                </table>
            </div>

            <h4>7.3 Example Calculation</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>For q = √(L × K) = L<sup>0.5</sup>K<sup>0.5</sup>:</strong></p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Step 1:</strong> Find MP<sub>L</sub></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace;">MP<sub>L</sub> = ∂q/∂L = 0.5 × L<sup>-0.5</sup> × K<sup>0.5</sup> = 0.5 × K/√(LK) = 0.5 × √(K/L)</p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Step 2:</strong> Find MP<sub>K</sub></p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace;">MP<sub>K</sub> = ∂q/∂K = 0.5 × L<sup>0.5</sup> × K<sup>-0.5</sup> = 0.5 × L/√(LK) = 0.5 × √(L/K)</p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Step 3:</strong> Calculate MRTS</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace;">MRTS = -MP<sub>L</sub>/MP<sub>K</sub> = -[0.5√(K/L)] / [0.5√(L/K)]</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.2rem; background: #eff6ff; padding: 0.5rem; border-radius: 4px; margin-top: 0.5rem;">
                    MRTS = -K/L
                </p>
                
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"For this production function, that's the marginal rate of technical substitution."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>7.4 Interpreting MRTS Along an Isoquant</h4>

            <div style="text-align: center; margin: 1.5rem 0;">
                <img src="/assets/images/fig5-3-mrts.png" alt="Figure 5-3: Isoquants and MRTS" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 8px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.1);">
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem; margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Figure 5-3:</strong> Isoquants and MRTS. Points A(1,4), B(2,2), C(4,1) show how MRTS changes along the isoquant.</p>

                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Point</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Situation</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">K/L</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">MRTS</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Interpretation</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Point A<br>(Upper left)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Little L, lots of K<br><em>e.g., 1 worker, 4 shovels</em></td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">4/1 = 4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">-4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Willing to give up 4 units of K for 1 more L</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Point B<br>(Middle)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Equal L and K<br><em>e.g., 2 workers, 2 shovels</em></td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">2/2 = 1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">-1</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Willing to trade 1:1</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Point C<br>(Lower right)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Lots of L, little K<br><em>e.g., 4 workers, 1 shovel</em></td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">1/4 = 0.25</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">-1/4</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Willing to give up only 1/4 K for 1 more L</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Intuition:</strong></p>
                <p><em>"When you've got four shovels and one worker, you'd happily give up shovels to get more workers. So you want to move to the right."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"Likewise, at point C, you've got four workers and one shovel. That's doing you no good. You'd happily give up a worker to get a shovel, so you want to move to the left."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Why?</strong> Because marginal products are diminishing! When you have lots of something, its marginal product is low, so you're willing to trade it away.</p>
            </div>

            <h4>7.5 Diminishing MRTS</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p>As you move along an isoquant from upper-left to lower-right:</p>
                <ul>
                    <li>L increases, K decreases</li>
                    <li>MP<sub>L</sub> decreases (diminishing returns to L)</li>
                    <li>MP<sub>K</sub> increases (less K means higher marginal product)</li>
                    <li>|MRTS| = MP<sub>L</sub>/MP<sub>K</sub> decreases</li>
                </ul>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;">This is why isoquants are <strong>convex to the origin</strong>!</p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- 8. Returns to Scale -->
    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">8. Returns to Scale</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Note:</strong> <em>"I'm going to talk about two things which are not parallel to consumer theory."</em></p>
                <p><em>"Producer theory is one step harder than consumer theory. It's all the fun stuff for consumer theory plus one additional step."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>8.1 The Question</h4>
            <div style="background: #eff6ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Returns to Scale:</strong> What happens when we increase <em>all</em> inputs proportionally?</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"When you say in your mind, 'what happens when a firm gets bigger?', you're thinking, what happens when everything increases? When all inputs increase proportionally is our notion of how a firm gets bigger."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>8.2 Formal Definition</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p>Consider multiplying all inputs by a factor t > 1:</p>
                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; font-size: 1.1rem;">
                    f(tL, tK) compared to t × f(L, K)
                </p>
            </div>

            <h4>8.3 Three Types of Returns to Scale</h4>
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Type</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Condition</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Meaning</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Example</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Constant Returns (CRS)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">f(2L, 2K) = 2f(L,K)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Double inputs → Double output</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;"><em>"Sort of a natural intuition"</em></td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Decreasing Returns (DRS)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">f(2L, 2K) < 2f(L,K)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Double inputs → Less than 2× output</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Crowding, coordination problems</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Increasing Returns (IRS)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-family: monospace;">f(2L, 2K) > 2f(L,K)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Double inputs → More than 2× output</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Specialization benefits</td>
                </tr>
            </table>

            <h4>8.4 Example: q = √(L × K)</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p>Let's check: f(2L, 2K) = √(2L × 2K) = √(4LK) = 2√(LK) = 2f(L, K)</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Result:</strong> This production function exhibits <strong>constant returns to scale</strong>!</p>
            </div>

            <h4>8.5 Why Decreasing Returns?</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Think about the idea that basically, if you're still trying to dig a hole, there's one guy with one shovel, you go two guys with two shovels, they're in each other's way, they can't quite work as efficiently as one guy with one shovel because you're still trying to dig one hole."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Causes of DRS:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Coordination problems</li>
                    <li>Communication difficulties</li>
                    <li>Workers getting in each other's way</li>
                    <li>Management challenges</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>8.6 Why Increasing Returns?</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0fdf4; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"That might arise through something like the fact that when the firm gets bigger, it can specialize more. So maybe as a firm gets bigger and bigger, it can get better and better at what it does, and production can become more and more efficient."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Causes of IRS:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Specialization and division of labor</li>
                    <li>Bulk purchasing discounts</li>
                    <li>Fixed costs spread over more output</li>
                    <li>Network effects</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>8.7 Why This Matters: The One-Firm Economy</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"This is an important concept because basically, this is how we think about economies growing. This is the core of any model of economic growth."</em></p>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Key Insight:</strong></p>
                <p><em>"Typically, economists do not believe that there is everywhere and always increasing returns to scale. We typically believe returns to scale may increase initially as you specialize as a firm, but eventually you have to decrease."</em></p>
                
                <div style="background: white; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin-top: 1rem;">
                    <p><strong>The Crucial Question:</strong></p>
                    <p><em>"And the reason we believe that, or at least believed that, was that if there was always and forever increasing returns to scale, how many firms would there be in the economy?"</em></p>
                    <p style="text-align: center; font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: bold; color: #dc2626; margin: 0.5rem 0;">ONE.</p>
                    <p><em>"Because it could always just do better by getting bigger and bigger and bigger."</em></p>
                </div>
            </div>

            <h4>8.8 Modern Complication: Tech Giants</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Now it turns out, for search engines, maybe that's not such a bad description. We'll see in the trial that's going on now where Google may get broken up."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"So it may be that increasing returns to scale may last longer than we thought as economists."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>The pattern economists expect:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Initially: Increasing returns (specialization benefits)</li>
                    <li>Eventually: Decreasing returns (coordination problems dominate)</li>
                </ul>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"But typically, we think of firms as maybe initially having increasing returns to scale, and then eventually as they mature, having decreasing returns to scale."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>8.9 Returns to Scale vs Diminishing Marginal Product</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Important Distinction:</strong></p>
                <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                    <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;"></th>
                        <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Diminishing MP</th>
                        <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Returns to Scale</th>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">What changes?</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">ONE input (others fixed)</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">ALL inputs (proportionally)</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Time frame</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Short-run</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Long-run</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Can coexist?</td>
                        <td colspan="2" style="padding: 0.75rem;">Yes! A function can have diminishing MP but constant or increasing returns to scale</td>
                    </tr>
                </table>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

        <h2 class="section-title">9. Productivity: The Missing Piece</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"One of the most exciting topics in economics is thinking about productivity. One of the most famous—and the idea of productivity comes actually from a very famous application from 1798 of the idea of decreasing returns to scale or diminishing marginal product."</em></p>

            <h4>9.1 The Malthusian Prediction (1798)</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef2f2; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><strong>Thomas Malthus's Argument:</strong></p>
                <ol style="margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                    <li><em>"Think about the production of agriculture. There's essentially two inputs: people and land."</em></li>
                    <li><em>"The difference between capital and land is land is always fixed. Even in the long-run. There's only so much land. We got the Earth. Unless we go colonize another planet."</em></li>
                    <li><em>"There's a fixed amount of land, so that means that the marginal product of labor must be everywhere diminishing."</em></li>
                    <li><em>"That means that eventually, we're going to starve."</em></li>
                </ol>
            </div>

            <h4>9.2 Malthus's Grim Vision</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Malthus actually said that there would be cycles—the world would be marked by cycles of starvation."</em></p>
                <div style="background: white; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 0.5rem 0;">
                    <p><strong>The Malthusian Cycle:</strong></p>
                    <ol>
                        <li>Population grows</li>
                        <li>Unable to produce enough food (diminishing MP<sub>L</sub>)</li>
                        <li>Many people die from starvation</li>
                        <li>Population falls to sustainable level</li>
                        <li>Food production adequate → population grows again</li>
                        <li>Return to step 1...</li>
                    </ol>
                </div>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem; font-style: italic; color: #dc2626;"><em>"Really not a very happy picture of the future."</em></p>
                <p style="color: #6b7280; font-size: 0.9rem;">(Gruber describes Malthus as "a very, I think, probably pretty depressed philosopher")</p>
            </div>

            <h4>9.3 What Actually Happened</h4>

            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">

                    </p>
                </div>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"So we think about famines and starvation all over the world, remember, that's a political failure, not technological failure. There's never been a famine in a democracy. Famines only happen when corrupt governments don't spread the food they need to the people who need to get it."</em></p>
            </div>

                <p style="font-family: monospace; text-align: center; margin-top: 0.5rem; font-size: 1.3rem;">
                    q = <span style="color: #dc2626; font-weight: bold;">A</span> × f(L, K)
                </p>
                <p style="text-align: center; color: #6b7280;">where A = Productivity factor (Total Factor Productivity)</p>
            </div>

            <p><em>"He was right that the amount of land is fixed, but he missed the fact that the way we use that land will get more and more productive over time. He missed all the technology that was going to come. He missed the productivity improvements that we were going to see."</em></p>

        <h2 class="section-title">11. Four Key Questions About Productivity</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            
            <h4>Question 1: Why No Long-Lasting IT Effects?</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Why did the IT revolution cause this brief burst and then eventually kind of wear off?"</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"Is it because basically all our innovation is watching TikTok and not in making stuff? I don't know."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>The puzzle:</strong> Life is radically different from 25 years ago, but we're not that much more economically productive. Why not?</p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Possible explanations:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Measurement problems (we're not capturing value correctly)</li>
                    <li>Innovation is in consumption, not production</li>
                    <li>Benefits going to leisure, not measured output</li>
                    <li>Takes time for technology to diffuse</li>
                </ul>
            </div>

            <h4>Question 2: Where Does Productivity Come From?</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"I said it was free, it's not free, it's not magical. Productivity is through real things."</em></p>
                <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                    <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Source</th>
                        <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">How It Works</th>
                        <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Who Does It</th>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Education</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;"><em>"As we educate people to make them smarter, make them more creative, they'll think of cool new ideas."</em></td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Schools, universities</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Firm R&D</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;"><em>"As firms do research and development... they think up new ideas. And they develop new products."</em></td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Private companies</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Public R&D</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;"><em>"Like the National Science Foundation... The National Institutes of Health fund a lot of research."</em></td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Government (NSF, NIH)</td>
                    </tr>
                </table>
                
                <div style="background: white; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin-top: 1rem;">
                    <p><strong>Key Distinction:</strong></p>
                    <ul>
                        <li><strong>Firms:</strong> Do "applied research" - take basic ideas and turn them into products</li>
                        <li><strong>Government:</strong> Funds the "basic research" that underlies those products</li>
                    </ul>
                </div>
                
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><em>"So basically, there are things we can do as a society to increase our productivity—we don't just sit back and let it happen. Having more educated population, more investments in R&D. Those can really make a difference in terms of how fast we grow."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>Question 3: How Should We Spend Productivity?</h4>
            <div style="background: #fef3c7; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"Imagine you have a cool new idea that can make things more efficient. Take Henry Ford's idea. He could do two things with that:"</em></p>
                <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0; background: white;">
                    <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600; width: 30%;">Option 1: More q</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Make more cars (more stuff)</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Option 2: Less L</td>
                        <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Give people more time off (more leisure)</td>
                    </tr>
                </table>
                <p><em>"If A goes up, you can reduce L, and with the same amount of output. There's no reason an increase in A has to go into more q. An increase in A could go into less L, which people like. They like time off."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>The Hardest Thing About Teaching at MIT</h4>
            <div style="background: #f8fafc; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"One thing we learn in this class, the hardest thing about teaching a class at MIT, the single hardest thing is <strong>in the real world, people actually don't like work</strong>. I find this hard to believe, guys, but in the real world, people would rather not be at work than at work."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>The Pandemic Effect</h4>
            <div style="background: #f0f9ff; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 8px; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <p><em>"The pandemic may have shook this up in the US. The pandemic was the first chance that people had to actually be at home and say, wait a second, maybe I don't want to work that hard."</em></p>
                <p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;"><strong>Post-pandemic changes:</strong></p>
                <ul>
                    <li>Huge reduction in labor supply</li>
                    <li>Many people retired early</li>
                    <li>People aren't working as hard</li>
                    <li>People want better jobs</li>
                    <li>People want to work from home</li>
                </ul>
                <p><em>"So the pandemic really shook things up in terms of people maybe starting to value their leisure more than maybe they did before."</em></p>
            </div>

            <h4>Question 4: Who Gets A? (The Distribution Question)</h4>

    <section class="section fade-in-delay">
        <h2 class="section-title">Key Terms Glossary</h2>
        <div class="section-content">
            <table style="width:100%; border-collapse: collapse; margin: 1rem 0;">
                <tr style="background: #f8fafc; border-bottom: 2px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left; width: 25%;">Term</th>
                    <th style="padding: 0.75rem; text-align: left;">Definition</th>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Production Function</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">q = f(L, K); Mathematical relationship showing what output is technically feasible given inputs</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Labor (L)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Hours of work; price = wage (w)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Capital (K)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Machines, buildings, land; price = rental rate (r)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Short-Run</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Period where some inputs are fixed (typically K)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Long-Run</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Period where all inputs are variable</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Marginal Product (MP)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Additional output from one more unit of input: MP<sub>L</sub> = ∂q/∂L</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Diminishing MP</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">∂²q/∂L² < 0; each additional worker adds less and less (but still positive!)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Isoquant</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Combinations of L and K producing the same output; like indifference curve for producers</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">MRTS</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Marginal Rate of Technical Substitution = -MP<sub>L</sub>/MP<sub>K</sub>; slope of isoquant</td>
                </tr>
                <tr style="border-bottom: 1px solid #e5e7eb;">
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Returns to Scale</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">What happens when ALL inputs scale proportionally (constant, increasing, or decreasing)</td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem; font-weight: 600;">Productivity (A)</td>
                    <td style="padding: 0.75rem;">Technology factor that makes inputs more efficient; q = A × f(L,K)</td>
                </tr>
            </table>
        </div>
